Hand-wired build: 4 small steps

Each step is one small circuit. Finish a step, tick its connections in the checklist at the bottom, then move on. Every diagram uses the same two rails: +12 V on top and GND at the bottom.

Never let a +12 V wire touch an ESP32 pin. The ESP32 only takes 3.3 V on its pins (5 V on VIN). Keep the 12 V supply unplugged while you wire.

1

Power

12 V for the coin acceptor, 5 V for the ESP32.

  1. Set the LM2596 first. Connect only its IN+ and IN− to 12 V, put a multimeter on OUT+/OUT−, and turn the small screw on the blue pot until it reads 5.0 V. Unplug 12 V.
  2. Supply +V → +12 V rail. Supply −V → GND rail.
  3. Coin acceptor RED → +12 V. BLACK → GND.
  4. LM2596 IN+ → +12 V, IN− and OUT− → GND.
  5. LM2596 OUT+ → ESP32 VIN (may be labelled 5V). ESP32 GND → GND.

Why: a new LM2596 can output almost 12 V, which kills an ESP32 instantly.

2

Coin signal

Coin pulses reach the ESP32 through the optocoupler, never directly.

  1. R1 (10 kΩ): one leg to +12 V, the other leg is the COIN point.
  2. Coin acceptor WHITE wire → COIN point.
  3. COIN point → EL817 pin 2.
  4. R2 (1 kΩ): +12 V → EL817 pin 1.
  5. EL817 pin 3 → GND.
  6. R3 (5.1 kΩ): EL817 pin 4 → ESP32 3V3.
  7. R4 (10 kΩ): EL817 pin 4 → ESP32 D18.

Finding pin 1: the EL817 has a small dot or notch. The pin next to the dot is pin 1; counting goes down that side (2), then up the other side (3, 4).

3

Coin lock (SET pin)

Keeps the slot rejecting coins until someone taps Insert Coin.

  1. Find the 2-pin SET header on the back of the Allan, below the speed switch.
  2. ESP32 D23 → upper SET pin (one Dupont female-female jumper works).
  3. R8 (10 kΩ): from that same upper SET pin → GND.

Why R8 matters: if the ESP32 restarts or loses power, R8 keeps the slot locked so it rejects coins instead of swallowing them with no credit.

4

Slot light (optional)

The LED frame lights up while the slot is open.

  1. LED frame RED → +12 V. LED frame BLACK → 2N2222A C (collector).
  2. 2N2222A E (emitter) → GND.
  3. R6 (1 kΩ): ESP32 D19 → 2N2222A B (base).
  4. R7 (10 kΩ): 2N2222A B → GND.

Transistor legs: hold it with the flat side facing you, legs down. Most 2N2222A are E · B · C from left to right, but some brands differ, so check the seller's datasheet.

Don't swap the frame wires: the frame is polarity-sensitive.

⚡

Before you plug in 12 V

  • LM2596 reads 5.0 V on its own, before it's connected to the ESP32.
  • No +12 V wire touches an ESP32 pin. Only VIN (5 V), 3V3, D18, D19, D23 and GND are used.
  • Every GND is joined: supply −V, acceptor BLACK, LM2596, EL817 pin 3, ESP32 GND, R7, R8, transistor E.
  • EL817 is the right way round (dot = pin 1).
  • Acceptor switches: output NO, speed SLOW, coins programmed so pulses = pesos.

Then flash the ESP32 and run the tests in the owner guide.

Pin labels vary by board: D18 may be printed as G18 or IO18. D18, D19 and D23 sit on the same side of most 38-pin ESP32 boards.
